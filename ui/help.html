How to use Draftmeter

Start Draftmeter, then ride in Zwift. The overlay fills in a few seconds after you start pedalling.

Shortcuts

⌘⇧D · Ctrl+Shift+DLock / unlock the overlay. Unlocked (dashed border): drag and resize it. Locked: clicks go through to Zwift.
⌘⇧H · Ctrl+Shift+HHide / show the overlay.
⌘QQuit Draftmeter (Mac). Or right-click its Dock icon → Quit. On Windows: right-click the tray icon → Quit, or close the overlay from the taskbar.

Draftmeter has a Dock icon (Mac) or a taskbar button (Windows). Everything else is in the Draftmeter menu: the gauge icon in the menu bar on Mac (it shows your live draft), the system tray on Windows. From there you can show or hide each panel, reset ride stats, open settings, or quit.

The panels

Draft gauge: watts you're saving right now by sitting in someone's slipstream, as calculated by Zwift. Expect 50–150 W or more in a fast bunch on the flat, and single digits on a climb (drafting barely helps at low speed).

Avg draft W · kJ saved · In draft: since you started Draftmeter (or pressed Reset ride stats). "In draft" is the share of your moving time with at least 10 W of draft.

Climb ahead: the elevation of the next 5 km. Climbs are shown in orange, for example Climb in 1.2 km · 1.5 km @ 4.5% · +68 m. On a Zwift route it follows the route; otherwise it follows the road you're on. Brand-new roads are learned from other riders, so the panel may be empty the first time you ride one.

Nearby riders: riders within 60 s. Blue −2.1s = ahead of you, red +1.4s = behind. In a race, only your own category is shown.

  • W/kg: their average over the last minute. Who's on the limit right now?
  • Draft: what they're saving. A rider at 0 W is pulling or alone in the wind.
  • Battery: an estimate of how much "attack energy" (W′, the reserve above FTP) they have left, from their live power and their Zwift FTP. 60%+ fresh 30–60% below 30%: close to empty. A faded bar means we've watched them for under 2 minutes; ? means no FTP in their profile.

Group battery: the average battery of the riders within 15 s of you (ahead and behind), shown with how many riders that is. Riders with a faded bar don't count yet, because we haven't watched them long enough. In a big bunch it can include riders that don't fit in the list.

Tactics banner, based on how drafting works in Zwift: following a wheel saves ~25% on the flat but only ~2–12% on a climb. So an attack on the flat lets the others follow cheaply; on a steep climb it sticks.

  • Blue: before a climb: move up, sit in. Start the climb near the front; don't attack yet.
  • Red ATTACK: you're on a steep part (about 6%+) and clearly fresher than the group (you ≥ 55% and 15+ points above their average, or they're below 35%), with enough battery to ride hard to the top. Short climbs (up to ~1 km): in the last ~500 m, full gas to the top. Long climbs: only in the final part (last 40%, at most 1.5 km), because early moves get reeled in.
  • Keep the power on over the top: after an attack, don't ease off at the crest; hold it into the first part of the descent while the others are still climbing.
  • Blue: wait: a long climb that's too early, a climb too shallow to drop anyone, or not enough battery yet (it tells you from where you can go).

It's a nudge, not an order. The battery is a model: Zwift FTPs can be outdated or set low on purpose.

Something not working?

  • Stuck on "Connecting": make sure you're actually riding in Zwift. The first connection can take up to a minute; Draftmeter repairs the connection itself.
  • "Wrong email or password": that's about the second account. Open Settings… in the menu and log in again.
  • Overlay hidden behind Zwift: switch Zwift to windowed or borderless full screen.
  • Don't run Draftmeter on two computers at once with the same second account, and don't use that account in another Zwift tool at the same time: Zwift allows only one connection per account.
  • Still stuck? Menu → Show logs & ride recordings and send the draftmeter.log file to whoever gave you the app.

About

Draftmeter is a free hobby project, not affiliated with or endorsed by Zwift. It uses Zwift's unofficial spectator connection with a second account; your own account is never logged in by it. Use at your own risk. Draftmeter is open source under the GNU GPL v3 and includes third-party GPL v3 code; its source code is included in the app.
